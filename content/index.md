<h3>Politics</h3>
<p class="scrap-epigraph">‘In our time the destiny of man presents its meanings in political terms.’ —Thomas Mann</p>
<p>How can I, that girl standing there,<br>
My attention fix<br>
On Roman or on Russian<br>
Or on Spanish politics,<br>
Yet here’s a travelled man that knows<br>
What he talks about,<br>
And there’s a politician<br>
That has both read and thought,<br>
And maybe what they say is true<br>
Of war and war’s alarms,<br>
But O that I were young again<br>
And held her in my arms.</p>

<div class="scrap scrap-poem">
<h3>Despair</h3>
<p>At moonrise a weariness comes over me,<br>
And I dream of trees holding up a sky,<br>
Beneath which I walk, seeking a lost road<br>
To the house of a friend.</p>
<p>And in this twilight of my brain<br>
I pray for a gate,<br>
Or a bridge,<br>
Or a tunnel,<br>
By which I may come to an end.</p>
<p>But there are no walls for gates,<br>
Or rivers for bridges,<br>
Or mountains for tunnels.</p>
<p>There is nothing to stop me,<br>
There is nothing to hold me.</p>
<p>Yet I must go on—<br>
For how can one rest<br>
Where there is neither wall nor hill nor sea,<br>
And where in the full darkness<br>
Little lights show<br>
Only to vanish as one approaches them?</p>
<span class="scrap-attr">Ernest Walsh</span>
</div>

<div class="scrap scrap-poem">
<h3>Dream</h3>
<p class="scrap-epigraph">—after three pints of Vanzeelli’s wine<br>
and a very great deal of conversation</p>
<p>I rose and walked sunward.<br>
Bolts flew to my eyes and burst.<br>
Time passed but I stopped him.<br>
He drew a century from his wallet<br>
And gave it to me.<br>
But I could not spend it—<br>
I gave it away.<br>
Thoughts from behind me ran ahead.<br>
They drove on into the heavens<br>
With the fury of rockets.<br>
They possessed the world for one moment<br>
Before the earth tugged me back to her bosom.<br>
I writhed and writhed until I lay awake,<br>
Listening to the city grinding up the dead.</p>
<span class="scrap-attr">Wilson Askew, Poetry, July 1933</span>
</div>
