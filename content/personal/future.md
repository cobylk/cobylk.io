For much of recorded history, man would use a knife and a dead sheep to predict the future. The Mesopotamian [bārû](tooltip: roughly, "inspector") would open the animal, examine its liver region by region, and read off the future according to a canonical manual of conditionals: *if* the groove called the "presence" is displaced thus, *then* the army will not return; *if* the fissure called [bāb ekalli](tooltip: the "gate of the palace") is split in two, *then* the palace will be scattered and its possessions leave through its gate.

The goals and structure of this post are, in many ways, similar. I hope to extrapolate signals we might see into [conditionals](tooltip: or more precisely, flowchart-y things) about the outcomes they imply; and I hope to predict the future.
#### I. before the singularity
I don't want to write at length about how long I believe it will take before humans develop advanced AI, mostly because this has been [discussed](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/ai-futures-model-dec-2025-update) [exhaustively](https://www.forethought.org/research/three-types-of-intelligence-explosion) [elsewhere](https://www.forethought.org/research/will-ai-r-and-d-automation-cause-a-software-intelligence-explosion), and because I think the extent to which my inside-view understanding differs from averaged [expert views](tooltip: here, I mean the rough span of views taken by people at the AI Futures Project, orgs like Redwood, frontier labs, etc.) is insignificant for evaluating most threat models.
